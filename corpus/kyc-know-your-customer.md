# Know Your Customer (KYC) — background note

Source: summary compiled from Wikipedia, "Know your customer"
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Know_your_customer), text available under
CC BY-SA 4.0. Retrieved 2026-08-29. This is a condensed reference for the
research corpus, not a verbatim copy.

## What KYC is

KYC is a compliance framework requiring financial institutions and other regulated
businesses to verify customer identity, assess suitability, and evaluate the risks
of a business relationship. It sits within anti-money-laundering (AML) and
counter-terrorism-financing (CTF) regulation. Beyond identification it aims to let
institutions understand a client's financial behaviour and exposure to money
laundering and predicate crimes (bribery, corruption, fraud, trafficking).

## Regulatory basis

The Financial Action Task Force (FATF, 1989) sets international standards that
jurisdictions implement domestically:
- United States — USA PATRIOT Act (2001) and Bank Secrecy Act; enforced by FinCEN.
- European Union — a consolidated single AML rulebook adopted in 2024.
- Australia (AUSTRAC), Canada (FINTRAC), Japan (Customer Identification Act 2003).

## The KYC process — four components

1. Customer Identification Program (CIP) — collect and verify name, date of birth,
   address, identification number before account opening; screen against watchlists;
   retain records (US: five years post-closure).
2. Customer Due Diligence (CDD) — verify identity, identify beneficial owners of
   legal entities, understand relationship purpose, build a customer risk profile,
   monitor for suspicious transactions. EU triggers: new relationship, transactions
   over EUR 10,000, doubts about existing data.
3. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) — for high-risk factors (politically exposed
   persons, high-risk jurisdictions, correspondent banking): examine source of
   wealth/funds, deeper identity research, investigate ownership structures.
4. Ongoing Monitoring — detect transactions inconsistent with the customer profile;
   file suspicious activity reports; refresh risk profiles, more intensively for
   EDD cases.

## Electronic KYC (eKYC) and automation

eKYC uses digital document validation, biometric authentication (facial
recognition, liveness checks) and real-time risk monitoring. Adoption has
accelerated with neobanks and fintech. Some regulators have formal eKYC frameworks
(e.g. Qatar Central Bank, 2023); the EU is exploring integration with digital
identity wallets under eIDAS.

## Criticisms

- De-banking / financial exclusion: compliance cost-benefit calculations lead
  institutions to close accounts; UK saw >450,000 personal account closures in 2024
  and services withdrawn from >140,000 SMEs in 2023. Vulnerable groups are
  disproportionately affected.
- Political misuse: some governments have weaponised KYC by labelling opponents as
  terrorists to trigger compliance-driven service removal.
- Criminal exploitation: extortion using falsified information designed to trigger
  compliance failures; AI-amplified disinformation makes fraudulent content easier.
